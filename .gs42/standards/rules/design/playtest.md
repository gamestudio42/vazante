# Playtest and balance

A design change is a hypothesis. Shipping it without saying what would
change your mind is a guess.

## Hypothesis

Write, before tuning:

- The player behavior you expect to change
- The pillar or loop beat it belongs to
- What you will watch (time-to-complete, failure point, resource left,
  a quote about confusion)
- What result would make you revert the change

## Evidence

- Observation is what happened. Interpretation is why you think it
  happened. Keep them in separate sentences.
- One play session can falsify a broken teachable moment. It cannot
  prove a balance target. Say which of the two you ran.
- Change one variable at a time when you are balancing. If you move
  three numbers, you do not know which one the player felt.
- Do not treat the designer's own best run as the target. Note who
  played and whether they already knew the system.

## Decisions

End with one of: keep, change again, or cut. A keep records the before
and after value and where that value lives in the project. A cut
removes the dead path instead of leaving it "for later" if later has
no pillar.

## What not to claim

Never claim a playtest passed unless someone played that build.
Headless tests do not validate feel. Report the gap.
