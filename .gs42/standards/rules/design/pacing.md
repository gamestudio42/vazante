# Pacing

Pacing is the order in which the game asks the player to learn, risk,
and recover. Difficulty is a result of that order, not a separate
multiplier bolted on at the end.

## Read first

- The first playable minute and the first time the core verb is required
- Any level list, tutorial scene, or onboarding note in the repository
- The failure state: what the player sees, what they keep, what they retry

## Rules

- Teach one idea at a time. The teachable moment is a situation where
  the new idea is the only reasonable way through, and the cost of
  failing it is small.
- Do not explain in text what the next thirty seconds can show. If you
  need a paragraph, the situation is not teaching.
- The opening lets the player succeed at the fantasy before it asks
  them to master it. Front-load the verb, not the menu.
- Difficulty rises by combining known ideas, not by inventing a new
  rule and raising the numbers in the same beat.
- Recovery beats are part of the curve. Constant escalation hides
  whether the player understood the last lesson.
- Failure must be attributable. The player should be able to say what
  they did wrong. If they cannot, the information was unfair, not hard.
- Do not gate the core loop behind a long unskippable introduction once
  the player has already seen it. Respect the repository's skip rules.

## Scope of a pacing change

Change the smallest sequence that fixes the stuck beat. Do not re-author
the whole level list because one encounter is unclear.
